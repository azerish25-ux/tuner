# Timbre glossary — ro

Status: AI-drafted and AI-reviewed terminology decisions; not human-reviewed or native-speaker signed off. Device terminology verification remains pending.

## 1. Locale decisions
- Locale/variant: ro; standard modern written language, regional-neutral.
- Register: informal singular tu. Existing mixed formal/informal instructions must follow this decision in new translations.
- Buttons: short natural action labels. Instructions use the selected second person. VoiceOver hints describe the result in third person; labels omit control type.
- Gender: avoid gendered user descriptions and agreement with unknown placeholders. Do not append endings to placeholders or Timbre.
- Quotation marks: „…”. Sentence case. Ellipsis is …; normal word spaces; no added punctuation.
- Digits: ASCII 0–9, preserving source values. Hz, BPM, XP, ¢, A4 remain unchanged. Counted nonplural labels use count-neutral constructions.
- Notes: interface placeholders supply notes; lesson letters and chord symbols remain as written, including B, ♯, ♭. Never replace spelled accidentals with signs.
- Legal Settings path: Setări › Cont Apple › Abonamente. Preserve automatic renewal, cancellation window, charges, free access and trial facts exactly.
- Apple features: retain official product names Apple, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, App Store, Apple Music, Game Center, Siri. Use existing localized system-feature labels from tables; do not coin replacements.
- Siri: natural commands; retain every ${applicationName} and other token. Next, Again, Slower must also work as commands.

## 3. Course and song names
Instrument courses use the catalogue instrument names. Songs uses the term above; ear training follows Ear training. Songbook uses the term above with the instrument name. Originals and Standards & Classics follow the terms above. Established traditional song titles use their established local title; otherwise retain the English title. Timbre Originals titles are translated consistently. A title in prose equals its lesson title; retain names and years in lyric credits and translate connecting words. Sung lyrics remain unchanged.

## 4. Words that mislead
Tune means bringing pitch to the target; a tune is a melody. Sharp/flat direction uses the existing flat/sharp labels (too low/high), not accidental names. Bar means musical measure, key means tonality, pick means plectrum/plucking or choose according to context. Lock means pitch confirmation, not a padlock. Take means recorded attempt, run a streak or passage attempt, not running. Chart means chord diagram/sheet unless statistical context. Intonation means pitch accuracy over a sustained tone, not saddle adjustment or speech stress. Practice means musical practice; Feel means vibration feedback, not emotion. Chop/chunk are muted rhythmic strokes, never a missed note.
