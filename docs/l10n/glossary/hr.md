## 1. Locale decisions
- Locale/variant: hr; standard modern written language, regional-neutral.
- Register: informal singular ti. Existing mixed formal/informal instructions must follow this decision in new translations.
- Buttons: short natural action labels. Instructions use the selected second person. VoiceOver hints describe the result in third person; labels omit control type.
- Gender: avoid gendered user descriptions and agreement with unknown placeholders. Do not append endings to placeholders or Timbre.
- Quotation marks: „…”. Sentence case. Ellipsis is …; normal word spaces; no added punctuation.
- Digits: ASCII 0–9, preserving source values. Hz, BPM, XP, ¢, A4 remain unchanged. Counted nonplural labels use count-neutral constructions.
- Notes: interface placeholders supply notes; lesson letters and chord symbols remain as written, including B, ♯, ♭. Never replace spelled accidentals with signs.
- Legal Settings path: Postavke › Apple račun › Pretplate. Preserve automatic renewal, cancellation window, charges, free access and trial facts exactly.
- Apple features: retain official product names Apple, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, App Store, Apple Music, Game Center, Siri. Use existing localized system-feature labels from tables; do not coin replacements.
- Siri: natural commands; retain every ${applicationName} and other token. Next, Again, Slower must also work as commands.

## 3. Course and song names
Instrument courses use the catalogue instrument names. Songs uses the term above; ear training follows Ear training. Songbook uses the term above with the instrument name. Originals and Standards & Classics follow the terms above. Established traditional song titles use their established local title; otherwise retain the English title. Timbre Originals titles are translated consistently. A title in prose equals its lesson title; retain names and years in lyric credits and translate connecting words. Sung lyrics remain unchanged.

## 4. Words that mislead
Tune means bringing pitch to the target; a tune is a melody. Sharp/flat direction uses the existing flat/sharp labels (too low/high), not accidental names. Bar means musical measure, key means tonality, pick means plectrum/plucking or choose according to context. Lock means pitch confirmation, not a padlock. Take means recorded attempt, run a streak or passage attempt, not running. Chart means chord diagram/sheet unless statistical context. Intonation means pitch accuracy over a sustained tone, not saddle adjustment or speech stress. Practice means musical practice; Feel means vibration feedback, not emotion. Chop/chunk are muted rhythmic strokes, never a missed note.

## 5. Lesson author decisions (unreviewed, 2026-10-26)
Informal singular imperatives throughout. English note B is preserved by project convention, although Croatian classical instruction often uses H. Interval-only Second/Third/Fourth = Sekunda/Terca/Kvarta; explicit finger labels are Drugi prst/Treći prst/Četvrti prst. Up/Down shared pitch and stroke directions use Gore/Dolje. Bridge shared song/instrument role uses Most; instrument saddle is sedlo. Pinch = istodobni trzaj dviju žica; banjo roll = obrazac trzanja. Hammer-on/pull-off are established guitar techniques; describe their action naturally in explanatory prose. Blues, swing and shuffle are established style/rhythm loanwords, not English filler. Exact-source names/notation are proposed individually and remain unapproved.

## 6. Detailed lesson decisions — author proposals, not approvals

### Pitch, notation and harmony
- Preserve source note-letter capitals and B, all chord symbols (E7, Em7, Cmaj7), Roman-numeral case and accidental signs. Spelled sharp/flat become povišeni/sniženi; do not inject ♯/♭ into spelled source. Prose key names are C-dur, A-mol, B-mol; spelled F sharp minor is mol na povišenom F.
- A pitched note is ton; a written/rhythmic note is nota. Root = osnovni ton; tonic = tonika, explained as ton uporišta; home chord = tonički akord. Key = tonalitet; chord chart = akordska shema in song accompaniment contexts, not a statistical chart.
- Intervals: sekunda, terca, kvarta, kvinta, seksta, septima, oktava, nona; major/minor = veliki/mali for intervals, dur/mol for chords and keys; perfect = čisti. Same-pitch comparison = unisono; formal classification may call it prima.
- Triad = trozvuk; diminished/augmented = smanjeni/povećani; dominant seventh = dominantni septakord; major seventh chord = veliki durski septakord; minor seventh chord = mali molski septakord. Progression = akordski slijed; authentic/plagal/deceptive/half cadence = autentična/plagalna/varava kadenca/polukadenca.
- Relative solmization is do, re, mi, fa, sol, la, ti, do. This course teaches movable do, so retain ti as degree seven, rather than silently converting to fixed-do si. Whole-string sung sequences are individually held candidates.
- W/H scale patterns retain their symbolic letters with the Croatian legend W = cijeli ton, H = poluton.
- Quiz answers shared by several contexts use every supplied role: The first/The second = Prvi primjer/Drugi primjer where gender varies; None of them = Ništa od toga; Either one = Bilo što od toga. Phrase ordinals (Treća) are not intervals (Terca), and explicit finger labels name the finger.

### Rhythm, instruments and techniques
- Whole/half/quarter/eighth/sixteenth notes = cijela nota/polovinka/četvrtinka/osminka/šesnaestinka. Matching rests = cijela/polovinska/četvrtinska/osminska/šesnaestinska pauza. Beat = doba; bar = takt; time signature = oznaka mjere. The English bar/measure synonym quiz explicitly explains both English labels rather than confusing takt with meter.
- Tie = ligatura, not the bowed-string slur = legato luk. Tuplet here = triola; accent = naglasak; syncopation = sinkopa. Offbeat is explained as između doba, na i. Backbeat is naglasak na slabim dobama, specifically two and four.
- Spoken eighth counts translate and → i. Sixteenth one-e-and-a → jedan-e-i-a. Triplet 1-trip-let → 1-ta-ta; the last syllable is explicitly završno ta. Swing vocables doo-ba/hum-pty are duu-ba; banjo bum-ditty is bum-diti. Vowel ah → a and loo → lu preserve pronunciation, not English orthography.
- Guitar/mandolin bridge = most, explicitly bowed-string bridge = kobilica; saddle = sedlo. Bow = gudalo, frog = žabica, rosin = kolofonij, scroll = puž, chin rest = podbradak, endpin = nožica, tailpiece = žičnjak, fingerboard = hvataljka.
- Bass = bas, banjo = bendžo, cello = violončelo. Open string = prazna žica. Down/up pick stroke = trzaj dolje/gore; bow stroke = potez dolje/gore. Strumming is naturally sviranje akorda or akordski trzaj according to action; fingerpicking = sviranje prstima.
- Chop/chunk = kratki/prigušeni akordski udar, never cutting. Crosspicking = križno trzanje. Banjo roll = obrazac trzanja; pinch = istodobni trzaj; forward roll = obrazac naprijed. Named hammer-on/pull-off/clawhammer and style names are individually justified where identical, with Croatian explanations.
- Section chips: Uvod, Strofa, Refren, Pripjev (refrain distinguished from chorus), Predrefren, Most, Provedba, Varijacija, Tema, Koračnica, Trio. Last march uses short Završni marš; last answer uses Zadnji odgovor; Sunrise chip uses Sunce izlazi. All pass mechanical source max_length checks; device rendering still pending.

## Independent review overrides

These reviewed choices supersede conflicting earlier proposals.
- A minor is the E shape moved one string thinner: fingers 2 and 3 on fret 2, finger 1 on string 2, fret 1. → A-mol je hvat E pomaknut za jednu žicu prema tanjima: prsti 2 i 3 na pragu 2, prst 1 na žici 2, pragu 1. — The original says exactly one string thinner. The draft says only onto thinner strings, losing the size of the physical movement. Restores one-string displacement without changing digits or pitch symbols.
- Does this end at home? → Završava li ovo na tonici? — The same prompt is used for both single-note melodies and chord cadences, confirmed in the curriculum sound arrays (for example gtr-3-spotlight and gtr-6-spotlight). Osnovni ton narrows home to a chord root/single note; tonika describes the tonal home for both melody and harmony.
- D minor: one note lower → D-mol: jedan ton ide niže — The lesson lowers just one chord tone from F sharp to F, not the whole chord by one whole tone. Jedan ton niže can mean a whole tone lower in Croatian; jedan ton ide niže keeps one note as the subject being lowered, matching the next card.
- A seventh chord adds a note that pulls toward home. D7 wants to move to G. Fingers: 2 on string 3, fret 2; 1 on string 2, fret 1; 3 on string 1, fret 2. → Septakord dodaje ton koji vuče prema tonici. D7 želi prijeći u G. Prsti: 2 na žici 3, pragu 2; 1 na žici 2, pragu 1; 3 na žici 1, pragu 2. — Home here is the tonic harmony G, not necessarily its single root pitch. The draft narrows the harmonic tendency to osnovni ton; tonika preserves the intended resolution without implying the added seventh itself must move to the root.
- A power chord plays only the root and the fifth, the note five letters up. Neither major nor minor, it stays punchy, even loud. E5 is the bottom of E minor. → Kvintni akord sadrži samo osnovni ton i kvintu, peti ton kad slova brojiš od osnovnog tona. Nije ni dur ni mol i ostaje prodoran, čak i glasno odsviran. E5 je donji dio E-mola. — Pet slovnih mjesta više suggests moving five places, which gives a sixth. A fifth counts the root as the first letter. Explicit inclusive counting preserves the intended root/fifth lesson; authoritative Croatian definition is the first-to-fifth scale-degree interval (https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/kvinta).
- Rock often builds with muted eighths, then lands on an open chord. Lift your palm for the big strum and let it ring. → Rock često gradi napetost prigušenim osminkama, a zatim stiže na neprigušeni akord. Podigni dlan za snažan potez i pusti ga da zvoni. — Open contrasts with palm-muted sound in this exercise, not with a movable/barre chord position. Neprigušeni makes the intended release of muting explicit; the following instruction confirms lifting the palm.
- The F barre is which open shape, moved up a fret? → Koji otvoreni hvat pomakneš prag više da dobiješ bare hvat F? — The draft question has incompatible predicates and no grammatical relation between the shifted shape and F. This natural interrogative preserves the same open-shape identification and one-fret shift.
- Travis picking, named after Merle Travis, keeps the thumb alternating on every beat while the fingers pluck in between, on the ands. → U Travisovu stilu, nazvanom po Merleu Travisu, palac izmjenjuje basovske tonove na svaku dobu, dok prsti trzaju između, na i. — Drži palac u izmjeni is unnatural Croatian and obscures the action. The revised sentence explicitly says the thumb alternates bass notes on every beat while the fingers fill the ands, preserving the named technique and timing.
- The roll turns around at the top: p, i, m, a, then m and i on the way back. → Obrazac se okreće na vrhu: p, i, m, a, pa m te i na povratku. — The repeated i in m i i makes the conjunction and fingering symbol visually ambiguous. Croatian te expresses and without changing either symbol or the sequence.
- No new ideas: the alternating bass, p-i-m-a, Travis picking and the ballad. Keep the thumb steady and let every note ring. → Ništa novo: izmjenični bas, p-i-m-a, Travisov stil i balada. Neka palac svira ravnomjerno i pusti svaki ton da zvoni. — Drži palac ravnomjernim unnaturally attributes rhythmic evenness to the body part rather than its playing. The new instruction says to play evenly with the thumb while preserving sustained sound.
- B7 is five letters up from E. Five letters up from A is E, so E7 takes its place. → Osnovni ton akorda B7 peti je po redu kad slova brojiš od E. Kad brojiš od A, peti je E, pa E7 preuzima njegovu ulogu. — The fifth must use inclusive counting; pet mjesta iznad suggests five offsets. Names the fifth letter counted from the root without changing pitch/chord notation (see terminology-evidence.md).
- Visit the four chord early → Ranije posjeti akord četvrtog stupnja — The four chord means harmony on the fourth scale degree, not the fourth chord in a sequence. Names the degree explicitly within the title-length limit.
- In a quick change, bar 2 jumps to the four chord and straight back. In A: A7, D7, A7, A7. It adds motion right from the start. → Pri ranoj promjeni takt 2 skače na akord četvrtog stupnja i odmah natrag. U A: A7, D7, A7, A7. To dodaje pokret od samog početka. — Clarifies that four chord is the fourth-degree harmony; četvrti akord could incorrectly mean the fourth chord in order.
- The four chord in A is D7. The quick change visits it in bar 2, then returns to A7. → Akord četvrtog stupnja u A je D7. Rana promjena posjećuje ga u taktu 2, pa se vraća na A7. — Uses the same explicit fourth-scale-degree term as the quick-change title/card; preserves the chord sequence and bar number.
- Keep your hand moving in sixteenths, but relax your fretting fingers so most strums are dead, scratchy clicks. Press down only for the hits. → Nastavi pomicati ruku u ritmu šesnaestinki, ali opusti prste na vratu da većina poteza daje suhe, šuškave klikove. Pritisni samo za jasne udare. — Drži ruku u šesnaestinkama is unnatural and omits the explicit continuous-motion instruction. Names moving the hand in sixteenth-note rhythm while retaining the contrast between relaxed muted strokes and pressed hits.
- Reggae: the offbeat chop → Reggae: kratki udari na slabim dobama — The full lesson explicitly places the chop on beats two and four (card and quiz), not between the beats. Here English offbeat denotes weak beats. Između doba contradicts the lesson; na slabim dobama matches its actual rhythmic placement without changing source text.
- The chorus, picked at fret 5 → Refren, trzanjem na pragu 5 — Picked does not specify fingers rather than a plectrum in this CAGED exercise. Prstima unnecessarily narrows the technique; trzanjem preserves the picked articulation while allowing the source’s choice of implement.
- D at frets 7, 7 and 5 has the root-position shape of your C at fret 5. Lower down, frets 2, 3 and 2 are the top of your open D. → D na pragovima 7, 7 i 5 ima hvat osnovnog oblika kao tvoj C na pragu 5. Niže, pragovi 2, 3 i 2 čine vrh otvorenog D. — Root position is the chord’s osnovni oblik, distinguished from inversions in this lesson. Osnovni hvat merely suggests a basic fingering and loses the inversion contrast.
- Roller Rink's loop is the one, six, four and five of G. Which chord is the six? → Petlja pjesme Koturaljkalište slijedi prvi, šesti, četvrti i peti stupanj G-dura. Koji je akord na šestom stupnju? — The one/six/four/five labels are harmonic scale degrees, not arbitrary chord numbers. Explicit degree wording removes the awkward akord šest and matches the immediately following G-major counting explanation.
- Count up G major: G 1, A 2, B 3, C 4, D 5, E 6. The chord on 6 is minor: E minor. → Broji uz G-dur: G 1, A 2, B 3, C 4, D 5, E 6. Akord na 6. stupnju je molski: E-mol. — Names the sixth degree explicitly to align with the corrected degree-identification question; all source digits and note letters stay unchanged.
- Which one is the open G string? → U kojem primjeru čuješ praznu žicu G? — Koji je primjer prazna žica G equates the example with a physical string and reads unnaturally. Ask which example contains the sound of the open G string, preserving the first/second choices.
- Which one is the open E string? → U kojem primjeru čuješ praznu žicu E? — Uses the same natural sound-example formulation as the open-G comparison, without changing the target pitch or open-string distinction.
