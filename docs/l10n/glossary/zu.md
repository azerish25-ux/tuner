## 1. Locale decisions
- Locale/variant: zu, standard isiZulu.
- Register: Informal singular, warm direct second person; avoid gender assumptions. Short imperative actions; noun labels; third-person VoiceOver hints. Avoid reader-gender marking.
- Typography: sentence case, normal word spaces, no double or edge spaces. Curly apostrophe where needed; single ellipsis …. Quotes follow shipped table (Nordic ”…”/“…”; Baltic „…“/„…”; sw/zu “…”). Digits ASCII 0–9. Preserve line structure.
- Units: Hz, BPM, XP, ¢, A4 unchanged; cents = Amasenti. Minutes use existing minutes entry; seconds s, milliseconds ms on screen, full words in spoken hints.
- Notes: preserve inserted note names and all placeholders without inflecting them. No lesson note respelling. Accidentals follow existing note-name entries, never confuse with tuner direction: flat = phansi; sharp = phezulu. Double accidentals repeat the musical alteration, not the direction word.
- Legal Settings path: Settings › Apple Account › Subscriptions. For languages without localized iOS system UI, retain the actual English path, not invented translated system menu labels. Apple’s iOS 26 language-support list excludes is/et/lt/lv/sw/zu; English system-path labels retained (https://images.apple.com/ie/iphone-16/specs/). In-app Settings retains Izilungiselelo.
- Apple features: preserve product names Apple, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, App Store, Apple Music, Game Center, Siri; use shipped system labels, never invent product names. Home/Lock Screen and accessibility feature names require device verification when first encountered.
- Siri: natural imperative, retain ${applicationName} and every named token.
- Brands: Timbre, Timbre Pro, Pro, ChordPro unchanged and uninflected. Keep [G], ChordPro directives, URLs, ›, ·, ×, arrows and all format tokens verbatim.
- Musical tuning terminology overrides: the drone-training Intonation is pitch accuracy, not saddle adjustment. da/no/sv Temperament means musical temperament (not physical temperature); use Temperering / Temperering / Temperering respectively when this source occurs.

## 3. Course and song names
Use the instrument-name anchors for Guitar, Ukulele, Bass, Violin, Cello, Mandolin, Banjo. Songs equals the Songs tab. Ear Training/Rhythm reuse existing feature terminology; Music Theory/Singing and Originals/Standards & Classics are specified above. Songbook reuses the existing anchor. Keep English song titles unless a firmly established local title exists; preserve traditional proper names and Timbre Originals titles. A title in prose must exactly match its lesson title. Lyric credits retain names and years, translating only connecting words; lyrics are not translated.

## 4. Words that mislead
Tune: tuning verb/tab versus melody noun; use separate terms above. Sharp/flat: tuning too high/low versus musical accidental. Bar: musical measure, never pub or toolbar. Key: musical tonality, never password/keycap. Pick: plectrum or pluck, but choose in interface. Lock: confirmation of stable correct pitch, not a padlock; locked content is separate. Take: a recorded attempt. Run: streak or complete performance, not running. Chart: chord sheet/diagram except statistical plots in Progress. Intonation: pitch accuracy over a drone, not saddle setup. Next/Again/Slower: one term must work both on controls and as spoken commands, using shipped anchors. Practice: music practice, not professional business. Feel: haptic sensation, not emotion. Quest: optional side goal distinct from the Today activity set. Challenge: friend competition versus cumulative lesson test; translate by context. Finger numbers 1–4 and open 0 remain unchanged. Loop A–B labels are markers, not note names. For hammer-on/pull-off/slide/bend/vibrato use established guitar-teaching loanwords, not everyday translations; I–IV–V and V/I chord symbols remain intact.

## 5. Lesson author decisions (2026-10-26; unreviewed draft)

Author: `/root/translate_zulu_lessons` (AI translation author, not a native-speaker certification).
- Preserve note/chord symbols, fingering numbers, musical counting syllables and sung lyric locators. Retention candidates are not approved invariants.
- Musical pitch: `ukuphakama komsindo`; a note can be `eliphakeme`/`eliphansi`. Do not confuse pitch height with volume (`amandla omsindo`).
- Pluck a string: `ncinza intambo`; ring: `khala`. Strumming across strings remains `ukushaya izintambo`.
- Fret: `i-fret` / `ama-fret`; explain the metal fret in beginner instructions rather than inventing an opaque equivalent.
- Chord: `i-chord` / `ama-chord`; major/minor remain musical loanwords, not translations meaning big/small.
- Beat: `isigqi`; bar: `ibha`; distinguish a pulse from a whole rhythmic pattern in explanatory prose.
- English mnemonic strings whose initials encode note letters are retained inside otherwise translated explanations and explicitly identified as memory aids; do not translate the words and destroy the initials.
- Song titles are names; translate pedagogical subtitles and descriptions, not original sung lyrics.
- Lesson duration names: quarter note `inothi eliyikota`, half note `inothi elinguhhafu`; eighth note `i-quaver` (plural `ama-quaver`), with the two-per-beat value taught explicitly in its introduction. Longer fractional calques are avoided in short lesson labels. Native musician review remains pending.
- Spelled-out accidental: `i-sharp` is introduced as raising a note by one fret; a spelled-out sharp note is `G ophakanyisiwe` etc. This explanatory lesson usage distinguishes an altered note from a tuner direction; it supersedes bare `phezulu` only for that sense. Flat note analogously `owehlisiwe`; keep source symbols exactly when present.
- Pitch/gesture shared options `Up`/`Down`: `Phezulu`/`Phansi`; no tense and no pitch-only wording. Bare `First` refers only to fingers, `Owokuqala`; broader `The first`/`The second` use neutral `Okokuqala`/`Okwesibili`.
- Neutral numeral answers use standalone counting forms (`Kunye`, `Kubili`, `Kuthathu`, `Kune`, `Kuhlanu`) or numeral nouns (`Isithupha`, `Isikhombisa`, `Isishiyagalombili`), avoiding incompatible noun-class inflection across shared questions.
- `and` and `one-and-two` are rhythmic counting syllables retained as instructional locators; surrounding prose is translated. Regular spelled-out whole-beat counts use `kunye, kubili, kuthathu, kune`.
- `riff` = `i-riff`; `shuffle` = `i-shuffle`; `sus` and `suspended` remain explanatory musical borrowings. Avoid literal physical translations of these named techniques.
- Physical instrument bridge: `i-bridge` in lesson instructions (the support carrying the strings); do not use a song-section interpretation. The independently split source is preserved. Song Bridge continues to follow the UI anchor by sense.
- `Timbre Original` in prose means original composition, not first song: use `Timbre iqambe …` with the brand as uninflected subject. Navigation references remain the shipped `Izingoma` / `Izingoma zokuqala` labels.
- Finger labels: index `umunwe wokukhomba`, middle `umunwe ophakathi`, ring `umunwe wendandatho`, little `umunwe omncane`, thumb `isithupha`. Keep p-i-m-a notation unchanged.
- `hammer-on` = `i-hammer-on`, `pull-off` = `i-pull-off`, `bend` = `i-bend`, `vibrato` = `i-vibrato`: retain recognizable technique names and explain the actual movement. A slide uses transparent `ukushelela`.
- Source-clarification receipts: zu-028/s011 explicitly separates the finger holding fret 5 from the picking action; zu-029/s024 renders short bend as falling short of target pitch, never short duration.
- Prefer `kubha` / `kumabha` for musical-bar locations, retaining `ibha` / `amabha` as nouns.
- Sixteenth note: `i-semiquaver` / `ama-semiquaver`, taught explicitly as four per beat; preserve `one-e-and-a` counting syllables.
- Triad: `i-triad` / `ama-triad`, explicitly defined by root, third and fifth. Inversion: `i-inversion` / `ama-inversion`, introduced by moving the lowest note above the others. `common tone` is introduced as `inothi elihlanganyelwayo` with the musical term retained in its definition.
- Nut (physical support at the start of the fretboard): `i-nut`; never food/nut sense. Arrangement: `ukuhlelwa kwengoma`.
- Ukulele `island strum`: `indlela ye-island` within translated strumming instructions; `chunk` = `i-chunk`, explicitly taught as a muted percussive stroke. `backbeat` is defined by the second and fourth beats; `swing`, `ragtime`, `Motown` retain recognizable musical/style names.
- Musical pickup/anacrusis is introduced as early lead-in notes (`amanothi e-pickup`); the bass's physical `i-pickup` is a separate hardware sense (zu-067/s017).
- Root/tonic: full `inothi eliyisisekelo`; in already defined bass patterns, short `isisekelo` / `izisekelo`. Fifth as a chord tone is `inothi lesihlanu`, not a finger label. Walking bass uses `ibhesi ehambayo`, explicitly defined as a new note on each beat.
- Bow: `umnsalo`; bow tip: `ichopho`; `i-frog` is introduced as the held component, never a literal frog animal. `i-rosin`, `i-fingerboard`, `i-tailpiece`, `i-fine tuner` retain recognizable hardware names with physical/action explanations. Peg: `isikhonkwane sokushuna`; chin rest: `isisekelo sesilevu`; collarbone: `ithambo lekhola`, located explicitly above the chest in the first posture explanation. These specialized choices still need native musician review.
- Bow directions `i-down-bow` / `i-up-bow` are explicitly defined as rightward pull and leftward push; do not imply vertical lowering/raising. Small bow strokes mean short travel, not slow speed.
- Violin source clarification at zu-076/s028 explicitly means horizontal, parallel to the floor (`ivundlile, ihambisane naphansi`), never holding the instrument at floor height. Bass zu-070/s006 and s022 clarify adjacent lower-pitched string as the thicker string, not lower visual position.
- Natural note (contrasted with a sharp/flat): `ongashintshiwe`, an unaltered note, rather than an unexplained nature metaphor. High/low second finger stays explicitly numbered (`2 ophakeme` / `2 ophansi`) with the physical finger spacing taught in prose.
- Slur: `i-slur` / `ama-slur`; explain it as notes in one bow movement (`umnyakazo owodwa womnsalo`), not one physical bow shared by players. Crescendo, Diminuendo and Piano retained as score-marking answer candidates, with their meaning already translated in instructional prose; no independent approval yet.
- Century clarification: `1700s` becomes `ikhulu eliqala ngo-1700`, the century beginning in 1700, not a decade. Source numeric tokens remain unchanged.

- Cello support spike: `i-endpin`, explained by its support function. Position shifting: `ukushintsha indawo`; first/fourth position: `indawo yokuqala/yesine`.
- Mandolin string courses: `amapheya`, with `ama-course` introduced as pairs, not curriculum courses. Acoustic beating: `i-beating`, explained as wavering when tuning mismatches. Chop: `i-chop`; crosspicking: `i-crosspicking`, explained with the actual strokes/grouping.
- Tremolo: `i-tremolo`, rapid continuous down/up picking. Current corrected zu-101/s013 explicitly sustains tremolo through the long notes (`I-tremolo kuze kuphele amanothi amade`), not one pluck. Original and amendment receipts retain provenance.
- Banjo rolls: `ama-roll`, including `i-roll eya phambili` and `i-roll yesithupha esishintshanayo`. Pinch: `i-pinch`, thumb and middle together; clawhammer: `i-clawhammer`, defined by nail-down motion. Banjo head is `ulwelwesi lwe-banjo`, the membrane, not a peghead. Drone: `i-drone`, defined as the recurring unchanged open fifth string. Bum-ditty syllables are rhythm notation, held as retention candidates.
- Interval quality: `i-interval yesibili/yesithathu/yesine/yesihlanu/yesithupha/yesikhombisa eyi-major/minor/perfect`. `perfect` is a musical classification, not a moral/quality judgment. Chord labels remain separately `i-major`, `i-minor`, `i-diminished`, `i-augmented`; explanatory prose teaches their construction. Dominant seventh chord: `i-chord ye-dominant seventh`, distinct from a minor-seventh interval.
- Solfège: retain Do, Re, Mi, Fa, Sol, La, Ti in musical answer sequences, with individual exact/normalized-source candidate reasons. In prose use u-do/ku-mi etc. Never interpret Do as an imperative verb. Scale degrees: `izinyathelo ze-scale`; unison: `i-unison`; octave: `i-octave`, all taught by their musical relationships.
- Humming: `cula uvale umlomo`; audiation: `ukuzwa engqondweni`, with the term audiation in its definition. Cadence: `i-cadence`, taught as a short chord pattern closing a phrase. Melodic dictation: `ukubhala umculo ngokuzwa`, with the actual multiple-choice task explained.
- Physical keyboard keys: `ukhiye` / `okhiye`; tonality is explicitly `ukhiye womculo` when both senses occur together. Enharmonic: `i-enharmonic`, defined as different names for the same pitch. Half step: `uhhafu wethoni`; whole step: `ithoni egcwele`.

- Musical rest: `isikhawu`; bar/measure equivalence is taught as `ibha` / `i-measure`, not a measuring action. Musical meter is not a unit of length; explanatory rhythmic grouping must remain clear.

- Time signature: beginner heading `Izinombolo zesigqi`, with `uphawu lwesikhathi` explained as two stacked numbers.
